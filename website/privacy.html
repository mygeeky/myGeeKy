Privacy policy

Effective 26 October 2026 · applies to the myGeeKy desktop panel, command line, installer and phone app, and to this website

In short
  • myGeeKy runs on your computer and your phone. There is no myGeeKy server, no account with us, and no database of users.
  • Your CV, your settings and what myGeeKy learns about you stay on your devices, unless you turn on sync, which uses your own private GitHub repository or, if you choose, a hidden folder in your own Google Drive.
  • Your GitHub tokens are kept in your system's secure credential store, never in files, logs or messages.
  • myGeeKy only reads public data. It never follows, stars, posts or writes anything on your behalf.
  • No analytics, no tracking, no ads, and nothing is sold or shared.

Who we are

myGeeKy is free, open-source software (MIT License), written and maintained by Alsamman M. Alsamman. Its source code is public at github.com/mygeeky/myGeeKy, so everything below can be checked against the code.

Your GitHub tokens

  • Read-only by design. The main token (or Sign in with GitHub, which asks for no permissions at all) can only read public data. Setup checks this and refuses a token that can follow people or change repositories.
  • To tell the two tokens apart, setup asks GitHub what a token can do, with checks that change nothing you can see. One of them creates a small unreferenced file object in one of your repositories, which never appears in it and which GitHub removes automatically.
  • The optional Signals token can write to one repository only: your mygeeky-beacon.
  • Tokens are never written to files or logs and never sent anywhere except to GitHub. Connect your phone shows a QR code containing your read-only token only if it is strictly read-only, only when you ask, and only for two minutes. The Signals token never goes into a QR code.
  • You can revoke a token, or Sign in with GitHub, at any time in your GitHub settings.

Sign in with ORCID

  • It only confirms your ORCID iD. ORCID shows its own sign-in page; myGeeKy never sees your ORCID password. It asks for the openid permission only: who you are, nothing to read or change in your record.
  • ORCID returns a token that expires after 10 minutes. myGeeKy uses it once, to ask ORCID for your iD and name, and does not keep it.
  • ORCID sends you back to mygeeky.org/orcid/, a static page that passes the answer straight to the app (on a computer, to myGeeKy on that same computer). It stores nothing and sends nothing anywhere else.

Google Drive (optional)

You can choose to keep your myGeeKy data in your own Google Drive instead of a GitHub repository, so your computers and phone share it. This option is being added: until it appears in your app, myGeeKy does not connect to Google at all.

  • What myGeeKy asks Google for. Only the drive.appdata permission: "See, create and delete its own configuration data in your Google Drive". This is a hidden folder that belongs to myGeeKy alone. myGeeKy cannot see, open or change any of your other Drive files, and does not ask for your contacts, email or anything else in your Google account.
  • What it keeps there. The same data the GitHub sync keeps (your settings, the interests myGeeKy learned, your badges and history, and, from a computer, your CV text), encrypted on your device before it is sent. The key stays on your devices (it travels between them by the QR code you scan), so Google cannot read the files, and neither can anyone else.
  • How it is used. Only to save your data and bring it back on your other devices. It is never sent to myGeeKy (there is no myGeeKy server), never shared, sold or used for advertising, and never used to train any model. No person reads it.
  • Where it is stored. In your Google Drive, under Google's privacy policy; it counts toward your own Drive storage. Google's sign-in token is kept in your device's secure credential store, never in files or logs.
  • Stopping and deleting. Turn it off in Settings → Accounts at any time. To delete the data, open Google Drive on the web → ⚙ Settings → Manage apps → myGeeKy → Delete hidden app data, and remove myGeeKy's access in your Google Account → Security → Third-party connections.
  • Google's rules. myGeeKy's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Who myGeeKy talks to

myGeeKy fetches public information directly from these services. They see an ordinary request from your computer or phone (your IP address and what was asked for), under their own privacy policies:

  • GitHub: profiles, repositories, followers, public activity, and your own beacon and sync repositories
  • Google Drive: only if you choose to keep your data there, your own hidden myGeeKy folder (see Google Drive above)
  • OpenAlex and ORCID: research topics and papers, and Sign in with ORCID if you use it; with Find my papers, the name and institution you type, as a search; Google Scholar: your public profile, if you give one
  • arXiv, bioRxiv, Hacker News and public news feeds (AI labs, journals, the tech press): headlines
  • Hugging Face, Product Hunt and pypistats: trending models, launches and download counts
  • PyPI: once a day, to see whether a newer myGeeKy exists
  • GitHub (again): every few hours, the public list of verified jobs, funding, conferences and learning resources (one file, the same for everyone); once a day the AI race (News → AI race: one file built from Epoch AI's open benchmark data, the same for everyone, nothing about you is sent); once a day the skill trends (Grow → Skills: one file counting skills in new preprints across the field, the same for everyone); once a day the datasets list (Grow → Data: one file, the same for everyone); Zenodo, NCBI GEO and Figshare: once a day, a search with your keywords for new public datasets (Grow → Data → New: only the search words are sent); and once a day the star and fork counts of your own repositories
  • OpenAlex key (optional): if you ask for your own free OpenAlex key (⚙ → WISEP, or You → Settings on the phone), the email address you type is sent to OpenAlex only, so they can email you the key. myGeeKy doesn't keep the address; the key is kept in your device's secure credential store.
  • Your own GitHub activity: if you're signed in with GitHub, My month reads your public GitHub events (what you pushed, starred or opened) to draw your month. They are read, not changed, and what is drawn stays on your device.
  • WISEP: every request to OpenAlex and the other open databases goes through one gateway on your device, which keeps their answers, learns which pages you open (kept only on your device) and asks again only when a fresh answer is worth it, within a daily budget. Requests to OpenAlex carry myGeeKy's contact address (support@mygeeky.org, not yours), which OpenAlex asks of apps. You see what it asked and what it learned, and can make it forget, in Settings → How myGeeKy asks the internet. If you ask there for a free OpenAlex key, the email address you type is sent to OpenAlex (only then, and only to them) so they can email you the key; the key is kept in your device's secure storage.
  • OpenAlex (again): if you follow researchers (Circle → Researchers), one search for their new work, about as often as they publish, and a search when you look someone up by name; the list of people you follow stays on your device and nobody is told; once a week, two searches to draw your co-author network (Researchers → Network) from your public papers
  • OpenAlex (again): once a day, the citation counts of your papers, and the newest papers citing the ones that gained citations (for Wins and You → Cited by); public data, kept on your device
  • GitHub and OpenAlex (again): when you build a protocol for a skill, a few searches for learning material on that skill (the skill's name and, if you give one, what it's for; nothing about you)

myGeeKy sends none of your personal data to these services beyond what a search needs, such as a topic word or a username.

Verified jobs, funding and conferences

  • Every listing is added by hand by myGeeKy's maker, after checking it is real. Each one disappears after its deadline.
  • The whole list is downloaded and matched to you on your device. Nobody (not the maker, not the employer) learns which listings matched you, which you opened, or what you voted.
  • Applying happens on the employer's own page, and only when you choose to.
  • The 🚩 on a listing opens a report you send yourself: a public GitHub issue with your account, or a private email to support@mygeeky.org. Nothing is sent unless you send it.

Protocols you publish

  • 📤 Publish on a protocol opens a prefilled, public GitHub issue in mygeeky/myGeeKy-ideas. Nothing is sent unless you press Submit there, with your own GitHub account.
  • It holds the protocol's stages and links, and the links you removed from it. It never holds your progress, your quiz answers or anything else about you.
  • myGeeKy's maker may turn it into a reviewed protocol for everyone, and uses it to train the protocol model.

What you choose to make public

  • Signals (optional). Your mygeeky-beacon repository is public. It holds your public encryption keys, your status, whether you are in quiet mode, your interest tags (you can turn this off), and the signals you sent. Each signal is encrypted so that only its recipient can read it, including who sent it. Signals expire after 90 days. Each of your devices (computers and, if you turn it on there, your phone) has its own key: the private half never leaves that device, and a phone uses its own Signals token, kept in the phone's secure store.
  • Calls for contributors (optional, part of Signals). A call you post goes into your public beacon: the name of one of your own public repositories, the short note you wrote, the skills you asked for, the level and time it takes, and when it closes (at most 90 days). Anyone can read it. Whether a call fits someone is worked out on their device, from their own interests; no one is sent your call or picked for it. When someone answers I'm in, their answer is sealed like any signal: only you can read who they are and which of their skills matched.
  • The Signals terms. Before you turn Signals on or post a call, myGeeKy asks you to agree to the Signals terms (be kind and honest, never post others' private information). A beacon that breaks them can be hidden from everyone's myGeeKy: the hidden list is part of myGeeKy's public curated lists, so anyone can see it.
  • Blocking and reporting. You can block anyone on Signals: their signals, calls and dot stop showing, and the list stays on your device. Report opens GitHub's own abuse form, because everything a person posts on Signals lives in their GitHub beacon.
  • Sync (optional). Your settings, CV text, scholarly profile and history go to a private repository in your own GitHub account, so your other computers see the same. myGeeKy never makes it public.
  • The 💡 idea lamp. It opens a prefilled issue on GitHub in the public myGeeKy-ideas repository. Nothing is sent until you submit it yourself, and you can edit it first.
  • Crash reports. If myGeeKy hits an error, it saves a report on your computer (in its logs folder) and offers to send it. The window shows exactly what the report contains, with your tokens, email addresses, name, GitHub login and folder paths already removed. Report on GitHub opens a prefilled public issue in the myGeeKy repository, which you submit yourself. No GitHub, or rather private? Email it opens the same cleaned report as an email to support@mygeeky.org in your own mail app, which you send yourself. If you do neither, nothing leaves your computer.

What myGeeKy never does

  • It never follows, unfollows, stars, forks, comments or posts for you. It only suggests, and you act on GitHub yourself.
  • It has no analytics, telemetry or advertising, and it never sends a crash report by itself: you see and submit each one. It never sells or shares your data.
  • It never asks for your contacts, location or microphone. The phone app asks for the camera only when you tap Scan from your computer or Receive a pack, and uses it only to read those QR codes: no picture or video is saved or sent, and no sound is recorded.
  • 📦 Packs (sharing part of your setup as QR codes) go from one screen to another camera only: nothing is uploaded, and a pack holds only what you ticked (keywords, topics, field, researchers you follow, a note), never your account or tokens.

This website

mygeeky.org is a static page hosted on GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, analytics or tracking. GitHub may keep standard server logs (such as IP addresses) as described in GitHub's privacy statement. Its fonts are served from mygeeky.org itself, so loading the page contacts no one else. The only outside service is the video player, which loads from YouTube's privacy-enhanced domain (youtube-nocookie.com) only when you press play.

Children

myGeeKy is a tool for researchers and developers and is not directed to children under 13.
